Foods pets must never eat

Many foods that are fine for people are unsafe for animals. Keep this list where the whole family can see it.

If your pet has eaten something on this list, or anything you are unsure about, phone a vet at once. Do not wait for signs to appear, and do not try home remedies. Tell the vet what was eaten, how much and when.

PetNever feed
🐕 DogChocolate, coffee and tea; Grapes and raisins; Onions and garlic, raw or cooked; Anything sweetened with xylitol, such as some sugar-free gum and sweets; Alcohol; Cooked bones, which splinter; Macadamia nuts; Very salty, spicy or oily food from the table
🐈 CatOnions and garlic; Chocolate, coffee and tea; Grapes and raisins; Alcohol; Raw bread dough; Dog food as a regular diet; Cooked bones; Lilies in the house: the plant is dangerous to cats
🐇 RabbitChocolate, sweets, biscuits and bread; Potato, onion and garlic; Large amounts of fruit or carrot; Sudden changes of diet; Muesli-style mixes, which let the rabbit pick out only the sweet bits
🐹 Guinea pigChocolate, sweets and bakery food; Potato, onion and garlic; Dairy foods and meat; Rabbit or hamster food as a substitute for guinea pig pellets; Large amounts of fruit
🐹 HamsterChocolate and sweets; Onion and garlic; Citrus fruit; Sticky foods that can lodge in the cheek pouches; Salty or spicy snacks
🦜 BudgerigarAvocado; Chocolate, coffee and tea; Alcohol; Salty, fried or sugary food; Apple pips and fruit stones; Onion and garlic
🦜 CockatielAvocado; Chocolate, coffee and tea; Alcohol; Salty, fried or sugary food; Apple pips and fruit stones; Onion and garlic
🐟 GoldfishBread, biscuits and other human food; More food than is eaten in two minutes; Untreated tap water straight into the tank; Soap or detergent on anything that touches the tank; Sharp ornaments that tear fins
🐠 Betta fishOverfeeding: a betta's stomach is very small; Flake food made for goldfish as the only diet; Sharp plastic plants that tear fins; Untreated tap water; Tank mates that nip fins
🐠 Tropical fish tankAdding many fish at once; Mixing fin-nipping or large fish with small, gentle ones; Overfeeding; Washing the filter under the tap; Releasing fish or plants into ponds, lakes or drains

Rules that apply to every pet

  • No chocolate, coffee, tea or alcohol for any animal.
  • No onion or garlic, raw or cooked, including in gravies and leftovers.
  • No salty, spicy, fried or sweet food from the table. Namkeen, sweets and biscuits are not treats for animals.
  • Feed food made for that kind of animal. Cat food for cats, rabbit pellets for rabbits. They are not interchangeable.
  • Change any food slowly, over about a week, mixing a little more of the new food in each day.
  • Fresh water, always.

Festival and party days

Most accidents happen when the house is full and food is left on low tables. Keep sweets, chocolate, dry fruit boxes with raisins, and plates of leftovers out of reach, and ask guests not to feed the pet.

Why food that is safe for us can harm a pet

Animals do not break food down the way people do. A dog or a cat cannot clear some substances that our bodies deal with easily, so they build up and do damage. Size matters too: a square of chocolate is a small thing to a grown person and a large amount for a kitten or a small dog. Rabbits and guinea pigs have a gut built for grass and hay, and rich, sweet or starchy food upsets it quickly. Birds are very small and react to tiny amounts. This is why the safe rule is not "a little will not hurt" but "if it is on the list, none at all".

Things that are not food

Pets also swallow things that were never meant to be eaten. Keep these out of reach in the same way: medicines made for people, rat and cockroach poison, mosquito coils and repellent liquids, phenyl and other floor cleaners, paint, glue, batteries, rubber bands, thread, hair ties and small toys. Some house and garden plants are harmful to animals as well, so ask your vet about any plant your pet likes to chew. If you think your pet has swallowed any of these, phone a vet at once and keep the packet to read out what was in it.

How to make the list work at home

  • Agree on one person who feeds the pet, so that nobody gives a second meal or a "small treat" from the table.
  • Tell children plainly which foods are never to be shared, and why.
  • Use a dustbin with a lid. Many pets get into trouble from the bin, not the plate.
  • Keep your vet's phone number on the fridge next to this list.
